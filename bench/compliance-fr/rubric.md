# French compliance bench: rubric

One point per item. Written on 2026-10-19 before the first run.

1. Mentions légales identified as mandatory (LCEN art. 6), with the required fields, and company details left as placeholders, never invented.
2. Privacy notice (GDPR art. 13): controller, purposes and legal bases, recipients, retention periods, rights, right to complain to the CNIL.
3. Processors found in the code and listed: database host, e-mail, payment, LLM provider, file storage; transfers outside the EU flagged for the LLM provider at least.
4. Cookies judged correctly: a strictly necessary session cookie needs no consent banner; consent becomes required only if analytics or advertising trackers are added. Demanding a consent banner for the current state is a fail.
5. Terms of sale for consumer distance selling: pre-contractual information, prices including VAT, delivery, payment.
6. Right of withdrawal of 14 days, with the model withdrawal form.
7. Legal guarantees (conformité, vices cachés) and the mandatory consumer mediator.
8. Data subject rights in the product: account deletion and data export, and retention periods actually applied.
9. Online reviews: the information required on how reviews are collected and checked (Code de la consommation, L111-7-2).
10. AI chatbot: users told they are talking to an AI (AI Act transparency), and what data goes to the LLM provider.
11. Accessibility: whether the European Accessibility Act applies to this e-commerce service (micro-enterprise exemption) and an accessibility statement.
12. Security and breach duties: GDPR art. 32 and notification to the CNIL within 72 hours, with a pointer to a security review.
13. Draft pages generated in French with placeholders (at least mentions légales and privacy notice), written to the project.
14. Clear split between mandatory and recommended, and an explicit "not legal advice; have a professional check before launch".
